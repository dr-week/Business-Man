## Product implication

These products focus on company data, prospecting, or customer-feedback research. Businessman can test a narrower workflow: put sourced market signals, counter-evidence, buyer checks, and editable economics into one decision brief. This is a positioning hypothesis, not a proven product gap or proof of willingness to pay.

Likely initial buyers to interview: founders deciding whether to pursue an idea, and advisors or incubators screening several ideas. Evidence of demand requires paid pilots and repeat use; competitor pricing only establishes existing spend categories.

## UX and validation

- Show source, date, geography, and known limits beside each claim.
- Separate observed data from assumptions and generated suggestions.
- Let a user inspect counter-evidence before recommending an action.
- Test whether users complete a brief and pay for it; do not infer demand from macro indicators, downloads, or public pricing.
